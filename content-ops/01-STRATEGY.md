# Strategy

## The one decision that matters most

Niche selection determines earnings more than volume, more than quality, more
than consistency. The CPM spread between the top and bottom niches is roughly
**50x** — finance/B2B content runs $25–50 CPM, gaming/entertainment runs $1–4.
Same views, fifty times the revenue.

It also determines *what "shock value" even means* for you. Shock in a finance
niche is a number. Shock in an entertainment niche is a stunt. They are not
interchangeable and they cost wildly different amounts to produce.

**So this gets decided before anything else gets built.**

## The three viable paths

### Option A — Sales / cold calling / B2B (RECOMMENDED)

**Why this one:** this repo is `Cold-Call-Coach`. You already have — or are
building — a product for this audience. That changes the economics completely.

- **Highest CPM tier.** B2B/sales sits in the $15–40 range.
- **You have a product to sell**, which is the #1 revenue line for a faceless
  page and dwarfs ad revenue. A million platform views is a few hundred dollars.
  A million views that sends 500 people to a sales training product is a
  different business entirely.
- **Faceless is native here.** Screen recordings, call audio, CRM dashboards,
  text-message breakdowns — the format *is* the content. No stunts required.
- **Genuinely under-served in short form.** Sales content on TikTok is mostly
  motivational fluff. Tactical specificity has almost no competition.
- **Shock value comes free and clean.** Real rejection audio is inherently
  high-arousal. "Here's me getting destroyed on a cold call" is shocking,
  honest, brand-safe, and impossible to fake convincingly. That's the ideal
  combination.

**Content pillars:** teardowns of real calls · objection-handling in 30 seconds ·
scripts that work vs. scripts that don't · the psychology of the first 8 seconds
(which, note, is the *same problem* as the video hook — the content and the
medium rhyme) · public "N calls in 30 days" series with a live scoreboard.

**Faceless formats:** call audio + waveform + captions · screen-record of CRM/
email · hands-and-notepad POV · text-message reconstruction · data animation.

**Risks:** smaller ceiling than broad entertainment. Slower follower growth. You
trade raw reach for revenue per follower — usually the right trade.

### Option B — Money / hustle / finance

- Highest raw CPM, largest audience, most affiliate inventory
- Brutally saturated, and the space is full of grifters — credibility is the
  entire game and it is expensive to establish
- Compliance exposure if you drift into anything resembling financial advice
- Only pick this if you have a real, specific, defensible angle

### Option C — Broad entertainment shitposting

- Fastest follower growth, lowest CPM, hardest to convert to money
- Highest ceiling if it hits, most likely to plateau at "big and broke"
- Highest policy risk, since shock is the primary lever
- Viable as a *second* account once the first one funds it

## Recommendation

**Run Option A as the primary.** It is the only one of the three where the
content and a business you're already building point the same direction.

If you want the shitposting outlet — and the reason to want it is real, it's the
fastest way to actually learn the craft — run it as **account #2**, on the same
five platforms, under a separate handle, entertainment-first. Use it as the
laboratory: test hooks there where nothing is at stake, port the winners to the
money account. Speed and volume on the lab account, precision on the primary.

## Positioning — LOCKED

**Name:** Cold Open — the film term for starting a scene mid-action with no
setup. Simultaneously what a cold call is, and the thesis of the page.

**Who it's for:** SDRs and BDRs, 22-30, 0-3 years in, B2B SaaS/services, US,
40-100 dials a day. Secondary: their managers, who buy for teams and will find us
through their reps. Tertiary: solo founders doing their own outbound.

**What they get in 30 seconds:** one mechanic they can use on their next call.
Not a principle, not a mindset — a specific sentence, a specific pause, a
specific order of operations.

**The point of view:** Cold calling is a set of learnable mechanics that live
almost entirely in the first 8 seconds. It is not a personality trait and it is
not a mindset problem.

**The enemy:** Motivational sales content. The grindset guys selling belief
instead of technique. "Just dial more." Every piece we make is an implicit
argument that the mechanics are knowable, and that telling someone to believe in
themselves is what people say when they don't know the mechanics.

**Proof:** real call audio, real numbers including the failures, and public
serialized attempts where the score is visible whether it's good or not.

**The CTA:** the Objection Pack — 12 objections and the exact words that beat
them. Email capture. Six of the twelve are already written in `BATCH-01.md`.

Why this positioning holds up: the enemy is real, we're right about it, and it is
inexhaustible. Every motivational sales post on the internet is a prompt. That is
what makes the tension renewable rather than manufactured — we never have to
invent a conflict, because we're already in one.

## Success metrics — first 90 days

**Revenue model: ad/watchtime.** The scoreboard is watch hours and subscribers,
not followers and not email signups.

| Metric | Day 30 | Day 60 | Day 90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-form uploads | 8 | 17 | 26 |
| YouTube subscribers | 100 | 400 | 1,000 |
| Watch hours (rolling 12mo) | 150 | 600 | 1,500 |
| Median long-form retention | 35% | 42% | 48% |
| Short-form posts | 150+ | 350+ | 550+ |

**Watch hours is the real number.** 4,000 in 12 months unlocks monetization, and
at an 11-minute video with 45% retention that is roughly 48,500 long-form views,
or about 930 a week. `tools/revenue.mjs` models it against your own assumptions.

Subscribers matter because they are the other half of the threshold and because
subscribers watch long-form. Followers on TikTok do not pay.
