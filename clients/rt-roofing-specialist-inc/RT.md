**One: his site takes 15.6 seconds to load.** 245KB, on a phone. That is the
whole finding and it is a real one — somebody with a leak at 9pm does not wait
fifteen seconds, they go back and tap the next roofer.

Our page has the same content and is **38KB with nothing loaded from anywhere
else**. That is not a marginal improvement, and you can demonstrate it live:
open both on your phone while he watches.

**Two: rtroofing.com is parked and for sale.** You were right, he did not know,
and it is still worth fixing. Anyone holding an old card, an old invoice or an
old truck photo lands on a page selling his name back. That is not a website
problem, it is a loose end — but it is his loose end and he will want it closed.

## What NOT to say

- ❌ "Your website is broken." It is not, and he can see that.
- ❌ "You don't show up on Google." His site is properly wired to his Google
  Business Profile — the link you found carries the GMB campaign tag. That is
  the most valuable link a local contractor has and his is working.
- ❌ Anything about hours or reviews. Our scan says they look absent, but a page
  scan cannot prove that and you have not looked yourself.

## The honest pitch

> "So — good news first. I went through your site properly on a phone and it's
> better than most I look at. It works on mobile, your number's tappable,
> you're wired into Google properly. I'm not going to sell you a new website,
> you don't need one.
>
> Two things I did find. Your site takes about fifteen seconds to load on a
> phone. Have a look now while we're talking — I'll wait. That's the one that
> costs you, because someone with a leak isn't waiting.
>
> The other's the rtroofing.com thing we talked about. That one's a loose end
> rather than a fire, but it's yours and it's worth closing.
>
> What I built for you is the same information on a page that loads instantly —
> thirty-eight kilobytes against your two hundred and forty-five. Want me to
> send it so you can put them side by side?"

That call either lands honestly or it doesn't. Both are better than the
alternative.

Then it is, and say so. *"Honestly, it mostly is. Keep my number for when you
want the speed sorted."* Send him the page anyway, free, and ask one thing:

> "Do you know anyone in the trades whose website is actually bad? That's who I
> can help."

A roofer who trusts you is worth more than a roofer who bought from you.
