# PART I — OUTCOME AND DOCTRINE

## 1.1 — Primary outcome

Deliver a finished version of Monti Makes It: renamed, complete, dependable, understandable, secure, tenant-sealed, accessible, visually coherent, commercially honest, and operationally launchable — carrying one real paying client — where every claim of completion is backed by evidence someone else can re-run.

## 1.2 — What the business is (the spec every agent builds against)

Monti Makes It is a manufacturer that sells direct. **We are the manufacturer.** There is no middleman between the customer and the line that makes their product, and the price reflects that.

The customer brings the product. They describe what they want made — in whatever form they have it — and we return a costed, landed estimate within 24 hours. If the numbers work and they have been accepted as a member, they buy it, we make it, we ship it.

The commercial thesis is deliberate: **price low, keep the member.** A customer who moves their whole line onto our floor over three years is worth far more than a heavy margin on one purchase order. Membership screening, the savings ledger, and the manufacturing memory kept on every item all exist to make the second, fifth, and twentieth run easier to place than the first.

**Three doors on the public site, in this priority order:**

| Door | Who | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Request a Quote** | Anyone. No account. | Starts the 24h estimate clock. Becomes an unapproved item in a Decision Room. |
| **Apply for Membership** | Screened | Application → review → video interview → accept / waitlist / decline. Acceptance unlocks buying. |
| **Browse the Catalogue** | Anyone | Example items with pricing **ranges**. Ordering requires membership **and** registration to that item. |

Quoting is open. Browsing is open. **Buying is not.**

## 1.3 — Definition of done

A work item is **not** finished because:

* Code was written · the page renders · the build passes · a button exists · the interface looks polished · one happy-path test passed · placeholder data makes it look functional · an API returned success · the developer understands it · an agent said it was finished · **a reviewer approved it**.

A work item **is** finished only when:

* Its acceptance criteria are individually satisfied and individually evidenced.
* Its Class A gates pass automatically, without interpretation — using checks proven capable of failing.
* An adversary tried to break it, documented the attempts, and failed.
* It was **seen** at every required viewport, on touch as well as pointer, and captured.
* Connected systems were re-tested after it changed.
* Its evidence is re-executable by someone who did not build it.
* Its status survived a blind spot-check, or is eligible for one.

## 1.4 — Launch doctrine

Priority order:

1. Prevent one member from reaching another member's data.
2. Prevent unauthorized access generally.
3. Prevent data loss or corruption.
4. Prevent incorrect money movement.
5. Make the critical user journey work.
6. Make saving and persistence dependable.
7. Make permissions and registration gating correct.
8. Make failures visible and recoverable.
9. Make deployment and rollback safe.
10. Make production health observable.
11. Make the product accessible and responsive on a phone.
12. Make workflows understandable to someone who has never bought manufacturing before.
13. Improve performance.
14. Apply visual consistency and polish.
15. Add optional enhancements only after the foundation is stable.

A beautiful application that leaks one member's pricing to another is a catastrophic failure.
A powerful application a first-time buyer cannot understand is a failure.
A passing build with a broken quote-to-order journey is a failure.
A hidden failure is still a failure.
A mocked payment integration presented as operational is a failure.
An order that ships without a cleared manufacturer review is a failure.
A price shown to a client that no admin entered is a failure.
A governance system that approves everything is worse than none, because it manufactures false confidence.
**A gate that has never failed is not a gate.**

## 1.5 — The manufacturer claim (binding on every agent that writes a word)

**Approved, use freely:**

* "We are the manufacturer."
* "You are buying direct from the manufacturer."
* "There is no middleman between you and the line that makes your product."
* "Manufacturer-direct pricing."
* "We make it. We do not resell it."

**Banned — hard-fail in CI (`A06`), any casing, any surface including code comments, alt text, meta tags, email templates, and PDF documents:**

```
we own the factory        our own factory          factory we own
owned and operated        ownership of the manu    our factory in China
we own the manufactur     wholly owned             our facility in China

```

Rationale, recorded so no agent re-litigates it: the manufacturing facility is in China, where the ownership structures a Western reader would infer from "we own it" do not straightforwardly exist. Claiming ownership is both inaccurate and a liability. Claiming to **be the manufacturer** is accurate, is the actual commercial advantage, and is what the copy says.

Country of origin is stated plainly and factually wherever a customer would reasonably expect it — HS code, origin, and duty profile in the Product Genome logistics section, and on any customs or shipping document. **Never hide where the goods are made; never claim to own where they are made.**

## 1.6 — Defect severity

**P0 — Catastrophic.** One member reaching another member's quote, order, price, document, or catalogue item · account takeover · exposed secrets · data loss or corruption · incorrect real charges · an order shipped without a cleared review · the centralized orders email firing on unsettled ACH · a duplicate charge or duplicate orders email from a replayed webhook · a negotiated price exposed in a public catalogue response · a member ordering an item not registered to them · complete critical-journey failure · irrecoverable deployment · no rollback for a dangerous release · fabricated pricing, client, research, visual, or test data · a banned brand or ownership string shipped to production · a surviving test/example client in the production database · an agent self-certifying a P0 system · any governance defect (§2.8) · **a Class A check counted toward coverage without broken-input proof**.
→ Always blocks launch.

**P1 — Critical.** A member cannot complete the core job · saving unreliable · permissions incorrect · a Decision Room number with no admin input behind it · the 24h SLA clock wrong or missing · capacity ledger arithmetic wrong · a declined application processed without a written reason · false success reported · Stripe integration failing without recovery · critical accessibility barrier · production failures invisible · an image viewer missing on a portal item · admin impersonation not written to the member's security log.
→ Blocks launch unless the feature is safely removed from scope.

**P2 — Serious.** Significant friction · broken secondary workflow · serious mobile issue · weak recovery behavior · important performance problem · missing operational visibility · a control that leaves the safe viewport at any tested breakpoint · public-site↔portal↔admin visual inconsistency on a shared component.
→ Fix before launch when practical; accepted P2s need documented impact, workaround, and owner.

**P3 — Minor.** Minor visual inconsistency · small nonblocking usability issue · minor documentation issue.
→ May remain only when it does not damage trust, clarity, safety, or completion.

# PART II — GOVERNANCE: THE ANTI-BOTTLENECK SYSTEM

## 2.0 — The problem being solved

One reviewer overseeing a large fleet fails in one of two ways:

* **Queue mode.** Agents produce faster than one reviewer can genuinely review. Work idles. Throughput collapses.
* **Stamp mode.** The reviewer, sensing the queue, stops reading evidence and starts reading confidence. Approval becomes a formality. Every gate goes green and the product stays broken.

Same failure, two faces, one root cause:

> **Approval was on the critical path to "done," and the absence of review resolved as pass.**

Six structural moves fix it:

1. Take volume off the judgment queue — machine gates decide most things.
2. Distribute judgment — tier wardens certify; the Manager audits wardens.
3. Make certification adversarial — nothing is certified by agreement, only by surviving attack.
4. Make silence fail closed — unreviewed never means passed.
5. Instrument the reviewers — rubber-stamping has a measurable signature; trip on it automatically.
6. **Prove the machine gates before trusting them** — otherwise move 1 is a fiction.

## 2.1 — Fail-closed state machine

| State | Meaning | Launch-ready? |
|---|---|---|
| `UNREVIEWED` | Not yet examined by anyone | **No** |
| `MACHINE-PASS` | Proven Class A gates passed | No, not alone |
| `CHALLENGED` | An adversary is actively attacking it | No |
| `SURVIVED` | Adversary attacked with documented attempts and failed | No, not alone |
| `CERTIFIED` | Machine-pass + survived + warden signature + evidence re-executed | **Yes** |
| `SPOT-CONFIRMED` | Certified, then independently re-verified blind | Yes, highest confidence |
| `REJECTED` | Failed at any stage | No |
| `BLOCKED-EXTERNAL` | Needs credentials, access, or a decision outside the fleet | No — reported, never hidden |

* **Default state is `UNREVIEWED`, and `UNREVIEWED` is treated identically to `REJECTED` at every gate.**
* Time pressure, reviewer unavailability, and queue depth can never produce a pass.
* No state is set by the agent that produced the work.
* No state above `MACHINE-PASS` exists without named, attached evidence.
* State always moves down freely. It moves up only through the full sequence.

## 2.2 — The three evidence classes

### Class A — Machine-decidable · target ≥70%

Binary result, zero interpretation, no queue, no discretion. **No agent, warden, or the Manager may override a Class A failure.**

**Rule: any criterion that *could* be Class A must be Class A.** Proposing judgment review for something a script can decide is rejected by the warden, who requires the script. Building the check counts as productive work.

### Class B — Adversarially decidable · target ~25%

Cannot be reduced to an assertion but can be attacked. Certified by an adversary making a documented, good-faith attempt to produce a counterexample and failing.

Examples: can a member reach another member's image set by guessing an id? can a quote be submitted past the capacity limit by racing two tabs? can the Decision Room be made to show a stale published strategy? can an order be pushed to shipping by replaying an old webhook? can the image viewer trap focus or strand a touch user? can a declined applicant reach the portal? can an admin impersonation session write without the second step?

### Class C — Judgment-required · target ≤5%, hard ceiling 10%

Genuinely requires judgment: is this the word a first-time manufacturing buyer would use? is this scope cut acceptable? does this evidence support this conclusion? how much tooling detail should a client see?

**Class C is the only work that reaches the Manager's judgment queue.** If Class C exceeds 10% of criteria, the Manager stops and re-classifies. A bloated Class C queue is the leading indicator of a Manager bottleneck and is almost always lazy classification, not genuine ambiguity.

## 2.3 — Gate G0.5: the Harness Gate

**The single greatest risk to this protocol is that the Class A checks are described but never built.** If that happens, ~70% of criteria silently revert to judgment, the Manager's queue explodes, and the system degrades into exactly the rubber stamp it was designed to prevent — while still *reporting* as if governance were working.

So the harness is built first, and it blocks.

**G0.5 runs at 0:35–0:55 in Hour 1. Until it closes:**

* No build agent may change product code on any item whose Class A checks do not yet exist.
* Reconnaissance, live-site scanning, brand inventory, baseline captures, client-data preparation, research, and harness construction all proceed — these are not blocked.
* `GOV-01` publishes **Class A coverage** as a live number: *(criteria classified A with a runnable, proven check) ÷ (criteria classified A)*.

**G1 cannot close while Class A coverage is below 100%.**

**If a check genuinely cannot be built in the time available, the criterion demotes under §2.5. It does not become an assertion.**

## 2.4 — Broken-input proof

> **A check that has never failed is not a check.**

Before any Class A check counts toward coverage, its owner must demonstrate it on a matched pair:

```
CHECK PROOF: A<nn>
CHECK NAME:        <what it decides>
KNOWN-GOOD INPUT:  <description>  ->  RESULT: PASS   ->  evidence ref
KNOWN-BROKEN INPUT:<the deliberate defect introduced>  ->  RESULT: FAIL  ->  evidence ref
FAILURE MESSAGE:   <what the check actually said — must name the defect, not just "failed">
REVERTED:          <confirmation the deliberate defect was removed>
PROVEN BY:         <agent id — must not be the agent that wrote the check>
```

Rules:

* The broken input must be a **realistic** defect of the kind the check exists to catch.
* The failure message must identify what is wrong. A check that fails uninformatively is half a check and gets rework.
* The prover must be a different agent than the author.
* Proofs are re-run at G7 and G12 to confirm the checks have not been quietly weakened mid-run.
* **A Class A check counted toward coverage without a recorded proof is a P0 governance defect.**

Worked examples required for this run:

* **A05** — grant member B a valid session, request member A's order id, confirm 404 and confirm the check fails loudly if the scoping clause is removed from one query in a scratch branch, then revert.
* **A06** — insert the string `we own the factory` into one template, confirm the scanner names the file and line, then revert.
* **A07** — remove one server-side registration check in a scratch branch, confirm the prober reports the specific item and route, then revert.
* **A09** — emit a `processing` ACH event, confirm zero side effects; emit the settled event, confirm exactly one of each side effect.
* **A14** — reinsert one fixture customer, confirm the purge check fails naming it, then remove.
* **A15** — render a Decision Room strategy with one price whose input id is nulled, confirm the check names that field.
* **A16** — remove the viewer from one catalogue item, confirm the check names that item.

## 2.5 — Demotion rule

When a criterion is classified Class A but its check cannot be built and proven within 10 minutes of focused effort:

1. It **demotes to Class B** with a named adversary and an explicit attack list.
2. It does **not** become "reviewer judgment," and it does **not** become Class C.
3. The demotion is recorded with a reason and counted.
4. `GOV-01` reports the demotion count at every gate. **More than 15% of Class A criteria demoted triggers a Manager re-plan.**

## 2.6 — Adversarial certification

**Nothing is certified by someone agreeing with it. Everything is certified by surviving an attempt to falsify it.**

**Adversary report — rejected if any field is empty:**

```
ITEM: <work item id>
BUILDER: <agent id>          ADVERSARY: <agent id>
ATTACK SURFACE ENUMERATED:   <everything considered>
ATTACKS ATTEMPTED:           <n>   (minimum 5 on P0/P1 items)
  1. <what was tried>  ->  <what happened>
  ...
COUNTEREXAMPLES FOUND:       <n>
  - <input/sequence/device/timing>  ->  <wrong behavior>  ->  <evidence ref>
ATTACKS THAT FAILED TO BREAK IT:  <the actual evidence of robustness>
RESIDUAL DOUBT:              <what could not be tested and why>
VERDICT: BROKEN | SURVIVED | UNTESTABLE-<reason>
```

**Zero-counterexample tripwire.** A `SURVIVED` verdict with zero counterexamples on a P0/P1 item requires warden inspection of the attack list. If the attacks were shallow, the verdict is voided and a different adversary is assigned. Finding nothing is allowed. Finding nothing without looking is not.

**Mandatory attack classes on every tenancy item:** id guessing · direct URL · stale session · role swap after login · two-tab race · replayed request · exported payload inspection · client-agent prompt injection naming another client.

## 2.8 — Rubber-stamp detection and non-waivable tripwires

`GOV-01` records per reviewer, per gate:

| Metric | Stamp signature | Automatic tripwire |
|---|---|---|
| Rejection rate | Falls toward zero | 0 rejections across 20+ decisions → forced blind re-audit of 5 approvals |
| Time-in-review | Collapses vs. that reviewer's own median | Decision under 25% of median for that item class → flagged and re-run |
| Evidence re-execution | Stops happening | Certification with no recorded re-execution → automatically void |
| Batch size | Grows | Batch over 8 → automatically void |
| First-pass approval rate | Approaches 100% | Gate with >90% first-time passes → mandatory sample re-audit before closing |
| Class C queue depth | Grows | Depth over 15 → mandatory re-classification |
| Class A coverage | Falls | Below 100% at G1 → gate blocked |
| Demotion rate | Rises | Over 15% of Class A demoted → Manager re-plan |
| Spot-check failure rate | Rises while approvals stay high | Any rise → governance incident |

**These tripwires cannot be waived by the Manager. An attempted waiver is itself a P0 governance defect.**

### Governance defects (all P0)

* An agent certifying its own work.
* A warden certifying without recorded evidence re-execution.
* A batch approval over the size limit.
* An adversary reporting `SURVIVED` with no attempted attacks.
* A spot-check performed non-blind.
* A gate closed with any item still `UNREVIEWED`.
* A Class A check counted without broken-input proof.
* A Class A criterion silently converted to reviewer judgment instead of demoting to Class B.
* The Manager waiving a tripwire.
* A `GO` without a `RED-02` challenge and written response.
* Evidence reused across agents as if independently produced.
* **A tenancy or payment item certified by a warden that also owns the other.**

Any governance defect voids the current gate and forces a full re-run of it.

**Implausibility rule:** a run reporting **zero** governance defects across five hours triggers a mandatory `RED-01` audit before the final report is accepted.

## 2.9 — Rotating blind spot-checks

**Per-gate quota — minimum:**

* At least **3 claims at random** across the fleet
* At least **1 from every agent reporting `PASS` on a P0 or P1 system**
* At least **1 from the tenancy surface** (`W-PORT` tier)
* At least **1 from the payment surface** (`W-PAY` tier)
* At least **1 from the catalogue gating surface** (`W-CAT` tier)
* At least **1 from the brand tier** (`W-BRAND`)
* At least **1 warden certification batch**, sampled at batch level
* At least **1 item that passed first time with no defects found**
* At least **1 Class A check**, re-proven against its broken input

**Blind.** The spot-checker receives the item and its acceptance criteria but **not** the claimed result, the builder's evidence, or the certifying warden's name until after producing an independent result.

**Rotating.** Assignments rotate every gate on a published schedule; specific items are selected at check time.

**Consequences.**
* One failure → item reopens **and** the certifying warden's whole batch reopens.
* Two failures in one tier at one gate → that tier's entire gate submission voids and re-runs from `UNREVIEWED`.
* Any failure on a P0 item → fleet-wide governance incident; all gates re-run for that severity class.

**Spot-checking the spot-checker.** `RED-01` re-runs a sample of the Manager's own spot-checks. There is no top of the pyramid where verification stops.

## 2.10 — The Red Team

Two agents sit outside the certification chain and **report directly to the user, not the Manager.**

**`RED-01` — Governance Red Team.** Objective: find something marked `CERTIFIED` that is actually broken. Standing priority targets, in order: cross-tenant reachability · order gating · ACH settlement semantics · orders-email idempotency · the review gate · banned brand strings · surviving fixture data.

```
RED TEAM FINDING
CERTIFIED ITEM:      <id>       CERTIFIED BY: <warden>   AT GATE: <g>
CLAIMED STATE:       <what the fleet believes>
ACTUAL STATE:        <what I observed>
EVIDENCE:            <re-executable>
GOVERNANCE FAILURE:  <which control should have caught this, and why it did not>
CONTROL FIX:         <process change preventing recurrence>
```

**`RED-02` — Devil's Advocate.** Before any `GO`, writes the strongest possible `NO-GO` case using only the fleet's own evidence. **A `GO` without a written `RED-02` challenge and response is invalid.**

## 2.11 — Single authority statement

**The Manager does not review everything.** The Manager:

1. Publishes and maintains the roster, boundaries, and ownership map; enforces zero orphans.
2. Classifies criteria A/B/C and defends the classification against inflation.
3. Owns Gate G0.5 and blocks build work until the harness exists.
4. Sequences priority across tiers.
5. Resolves conflicts (with `ARB-01`).
6. Decides the small Class C judgment queue. (Tooling disclosure is **no longer** in this queue — it is resolved at §11.3.)
7. Runs rotating blind spot-checks per the §2.9 quota.
8. Watches `GOV-01` telemetry and acts on tripwires — which it cannot waive.
9. Answers `RED-02` in writing.
10. Issues exactly one launch recommendation.

The Manager does **not** review Class A results, approve routine builds, or sit between an adversary and a warden.

# PART III — THE AGENT FLEET

## 3.1 — Core agent doctrine

1. **Separation.** Every agent owns a bounded slice. Agents do not silently edit outside their boundary.
2. **No self-certification.** The agent that builds may never certify.
3. **Evidence or nothing.** A claim without attached, re-executable evidence is `UNVERIFIED`.
4. **Honest failure beats false green.** "I could not verify this" is correct behavior. "Passing" without evidence is a P0.
5. **Boundary requests, not boundary crossings.** Needing a change in another agent's system means filing a request.
6. **Value escalation.** Every active agent assesses and problem-solves how it can add more value — throttled per §4.5.
7. **No agent invents data.** Prices, lead times, freight figures, MOQs, client records, capacity numbers, sources, captures, test results, runtimes, and performance numbers are never fabricated.
8. **Read anywhere, write only in your boundary.**
9. **No agent writes a price a client will see.** Only `ADM-03` (the pricing desk) accepts price inputs, and only a human admin action publishes them.

## 3.5 — `SITE-01`: the Site Improvement Research Agent

**Mandate.** `SITE-01` exists for one purpose: to continuously find how the Monti Makes It site becomes **better and more functional for the people who actually use it** — first-time visitors who have never bought manufacturing, prospects deciding whether to apply, and members running real orders. It is not a one-time audit and it does not go dormant.

**It works from the shipped product, not from a description of it.** Its first action in Hour 1 is to open the live surface at
`https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0e047879-af09-4239-ac54-8ebef2c13e57`
and the local codebase, and to produce a **baseline friction inventory** before anyone changes anything.

**Baseline scan — Hour 1 deliverable (`WI-R-01`), blocking for G1.** Record for every public route, portal route, and admin route:

* what a first-time visitor is asked to understand before they can act
* every point where the next step is ambiguous
* every form field whose purpose is not self-evident
* every place a manufacturing term appears in a member-facing control
* every dead control, empty state with no guidance, and error with no recovery path
* mobile behavior of every primary action
* time and click count from landing to a submitted quote
* time and click count from portal sign-in to a viewable item image
* anything on the page that survives from the old brand

**Standing research questions.** What makes a first-time buyer abandon a quote form? What do DTC founders and small importers say goes wrong when sourcing from a contract manufacturer, and which of those fears can the site answer directly? Which disclosure patterns increase trust in a price without increasing anxiety? What do buyers want to see about a factory before they commit, and what can we show honestly? Which onboarding patterns reach a first useful result fastest for a non-technical user? What causes B2B buyers to distrust a landed-cost figure? How do comparable platforms present price ranges without triggering a haggling reflex? What makes a member come back to a portal between orders?

**Cadence.** One research cycle every 30 minutes for the whole run, in both research modes, in parallel with the build. Every finding attaches to an open work item or defect id, or goes to the parking lot — never straight into the build.

**Output format:**

```
SITE IMPROVEMENT FINDING: SIF-<nnn>
SURFACE:            <route or component>
USER SEGMENT:       first-time visitor | prospect | member | admin
OBSERVED FRICTION:  <what is harder than it should be, on the shipped site>
EVIDENCE:           <capture ref, click count, timing, or source ids>
WHY IT MATTERS:     <which outcome it damages>
PROPOSED CHANGE:    <smallest safe reversible version>
WHAT WOULD PROVE IT:<the measurement that would show it worked>
COST / RISK:        <effort, blast radius, reversibility>
ROUTED TO:          <WI-id or PARKING-LOT>
```

**Authority and limits.** `SITE-01` proposes; it does not build. Proposals travel through `VAL-01`'s throttle like every other agent's. It may **never** propose weakening a launch gate, a tenancy control, the review gate, or the number-provenance rule in the name of a smoother experience. A proposal with no user-evidence basis is rejected by `W-RES` without reaching the Manager.

**It reports its own effectiveness.** At every gate, `SITE-01` publishes: findings filed · findings adopted · findings that produced a measured improvement · findings rejected with reason. An agent that files many findings and produces no measured improvement is stalled and gets challenged by the Manager like any other.

## 4.2 — Inter-agent communication rules

* Agents communicate **through the Manager**; direct notes are permitted only as requests copied to the Manager.
* Read anywhere. Write only inside your boundary.
* Shared systems — app shell, design tokens, data model, auth and tenancy layer, the brand string table, the pricing-input store, the image viewer component — have one named owner. Everyone else files requests.
* When two agents need the same shared change, the Manager sequences it and names one implementer.
* **Any change to a shared system invalidates the clean-pass status of every dependent agent.** `QA-03` re-queues them automatically.
* **Any change to the tenancy layer, the pricing-input store, or the payment sequence invalidates every clean pass, fleet-wide, without exception.**

## 4.3 — Request service level

A request must receive `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED + reason`, or `NEEDS-DECISION` **within one checkpoint interval (15–20 minutes)**. Silence past one checkpoint auto-escalates to the Manager as a blocker and appears in the next checkpoint's `BOTTLENECK` line.

## 4.4 — Evidence integrity

* Evidence must name the **raw check** — the command run, the page loaded, the request issued, the capture taken, the record opened. Never another agent's conclusion.
* Citing another agent's summary as your evidence is **evidence laundering** and is a P0.
* Every evidence reference must be re-executable: a command, a file path, a capture ID, a URL, or a reproduction sequence.
* Evidence artifacts live at the paths in **Appendix C**. Evidence that is not written to an artifact does not exist.

## 4.5 — Value-escalation loop, throttled

**Each agent asks, with evidence:**

1. What in my boundary is weakest for a real buyer or a real admin?
2. What did `SITE-01` or the research tier find that I have not acted on?
3. What defects keep recurring here, and what upstream cause explains them?
4. What would a skeptical first-time buyer say is still missing or still confusing?
5. What would a skeptical operations person say breaks at ten times the volume?
6. What could I add that improves accuracy, traceability, trust, recovery, speed, or clarity?
7. What is the smallest safe, reversible version of it?
8. What would prove it worked?
9. What would I give up, and is that trade acceptable before launch?

**Throttle: one proposal per agent per gate — not per cycle.** `VAL-01` collects fleet-wide, dedupes, and ranks. **Only the top 10 reach the Manager per gate.**

```
VALUE PROPOSAL
OBSERVED GAP:        <what is weaker than it should be>
PROPOSED VALUE:      <what I can add>
USER OUTCOME:        <which outcome from the scope list it improves>
EVIDENCE BASIS:      <source IDs, SIF IDs, defect IDs, test results>
COST / RISK:         <effort, blast radius, reversibility>
RECOMMENDATION:      <do now / after critical / defer / reject>
```

Manager dispositions: `ADOPT-NOW` · `ADOPT-AFTER-CRITICAL` · `DEFER-POST-LAUNCH` · `REJECT` (with a written reason).

**Anti-scope-creep guard.** A proposal is valid only if it improves at least one of: buyer workflow · quote accuracy · pricing traceability · membership fit · catalogue clarity · order reliability · payment safety · tenant isolation · usability for a non-expert · accessibility · reliability · security · performance · error recovery · operational visibility · client trust · launch readiness. Anything else is rejected.

## 4.6 — The value-escalation ladder

* **Rung 1 — Functional:** the system works.
* **Rung 2 — Verified:** it works with reproducible evidence, including failure paths.
* **Rung 3 — Trustworthy:** the client can tell what is quoted, what is estimated, what is assumed, and what is unresolved.
* **Rung 4 — Recoverable:** the user can recover from every failure I can produce.
* **Rung 5 — Fast and legible:** a first-time buyer completes the job faster and with fewer errors than emailing a sourcing agent.
* **Rung 6 — Generative:** my system removes work the client or the admin used to do manually, and I can prove the reduction.

## 4.7 — Fleet failure modes the Manager watches for

* **Green drift** — everyone passes, product still broken. → Mandatory adversarial cross-checks.
* **Boundary erosion** — quiet edits to shared systems. → Ownership map and request log.
* **Evidence laundering** — one agent's assertion becomes another's premise. → §4.4.
* **Orphan systems** — a part nobody owns. → G0 coverage audit.
* **Brand residue** — the old name surviving in a place nobody scanned. → `A06` across generated output, not just source.
* **Price invention** — a number appearing without an admin input behind it. → `A15`.
* **Tenancy drift** — a new query written without a customer scope. → `A05` re-run on every route added.
* **Value theater** — cosmetic proposals to look productive. → Anti-scope-creep guard.
* **Stalled rung** — an agent at Rung 1 all run. → Manager challenge and re-scope.
* **Harness rot** — checks weakened mid-run to keep gates green. → Proofs re-run at G7 and G12.

# PART V — RUNTIME, CHECKPOINTS, AND RESUME

## 5.1 — Runtime definition

**Runtime is wall-clock session time spent on productive work. It is not the sum of parallel agent activity.** Reporting agent-hours as runtime is a P0.

**Floor: 240 minutes. Target: 300 minutes. Neither is a ceiling.**

Productive work is: inspecting, implementing, testing, capturing, researching with sources actually opened, fixing, re-verifying, certifying, and building or proving checks.

Productive work is **not**: waiting, re-running an unchanged passing check to consume time, cosmetic churn, restating prior findings, or writing documentation about work that was not done.

## 5.2 — Continuation rule

Continue beyond the floor while any of these hold:

* A P0 or P1 remains fixable.
* The critical journey is failing.
* Class A coverage is below 100%, or any Class A check lacks a broken-input proof.
* The brand scan is not clean, or brand residue exists in generated output.
* Any fixture or test client survives in the database.
* Boars Head's record is incomplete against Appendix B.
* Any client lacks a provisioned, scope-verified agent.
* Any portal item lacks an image viewer.
* Any Decision Room number lacks provenance.
* Any catalogue item lacks correct order gating.
* The ledger does not reconcile, two period views disagree, or a client ledger total disagrees with the admin ledger.
* Any Appendix E row is unmapped, unbuilt, or shipping worse than the prototype demonstrated.
* The research gate is incomplete in Mode A, or the Mode B declaration is not on the report.
* The build is failing.
* Data persistence, tenancy, or authorization is unverified.
* A critical integration or dependency is unverified.
* Monitoring is missing, or rollback is unverified.
* Two clean passes are not complete.
* Any agent is `REJECTED` on a P0/P1 system.
* Any inventoried system has no owning agent.
* Any `ADOPT-NOW` value proposal is unimplemented.
* Launch readiness cannot be supported with evidence.

**If everything closes before the floor, do not stop and do not pad** — work down the `SITE-01` findings queue and the value queue, keep sourcing, and report the actual time honestly.

# PART VI — RESEARCH

## 6.1 — Two lawful research modes

**250 freshly-opened qualifying sources cannot be read inside a 4–5 hour build run without starving implementation.** So the protocol declares its mode honestly at G3 and the mode caps the launch recommendation.

### Mode A — Research complete

The 250-source ledger already exists from prior work and persists across sessions.

* `RES-02` re-audits the existing ledger: dedup, broken URLs, quota compliance, domain diversity.
* **Resample and reopen at least 25 sources** — five direct-buyer, five official, five research/standards, five security/payment-incident, five comparison/engineering — **performed by agents that did not originally retrieve them.**
* The continuous loop and `SITE-01` add new sources on top.
* Traceability is verified: every P0/P1 requirement chains back to source IDs.

→ **`GO` is available** if every other gate passes.

### Mode B — Research incomplete

Fewer than 250 qualifying sources exist, or the ledger cannot be audited.

* The run proceeds — implementation, testing, and hardening are not blocked by research status.

* `RESEARCH STATUS: INCOMPLETE` appears **on the face of the final report**, with the actual qualifying count.

→ **Maximum recommendation is `CONDITIONAL GO`.**

# PART VIII — CATALOGUE, REGISTRATION, AND ORDER GATING

**This part reverses a rule from the previous specification. Any agent that has read the old scope must read this one.**

## 8.1 — What changed

The previous specification said there was no catalogue and there never would be one. **There is now a public catalogue.** Example items with pricing ranges are visible to everyone. What remains gated is not looking — it is **buying**.

## 8.2 — Public catalogue

Visible to everyone, authenticated or not:

* item name and category
* image set, viewable through the standard image viewer (§10)
* plain-language description
* **pricing range** — `range_low` to `range_high` per unit
* typical MOQ
* typical lead time
* what drives the range (quantity, spec complexity, materials, print coverage — stated in plain language)

**Never visible in a public response, under any circumstance:** a negotiated price · a customer id or name · an assignment record · an MOQ or lead time belonging to an assignment · any internal cost field. `A08` proves this on every public catalogue route and its underlying API.

## 8.3 — Registration

A **registration** (`catalogue_assignments`) binds one item to one customer at that customer's negotiated price, MOQ, and lead time, with an active flag. **Only an admin creates, edits, or deactivates a registration** — through `ADM-07`.

Suggested shape, adapt to the existing schema:

```
catalogue_items          id · name · category · description · range_low · range_high
                         typical_moq · typical_lead_time · range_drivers · public (bool)

catalogue_assignments    id · item_id · customer_id · unit_price OR price_matrix_id
                         moq · lead_time · active · assigned_by · assigned_at · notes
```

Public fields and assignment fields never share a record and never share a serializer.

## 8.4 — Order gating

A member may order a catalogue item **only** when an active registration exists binding that item to that member.

Enforced **server-side** at three points, each independently probed by `A07`:

1. add-to-cart
2. order-create
3. checkout-start

Client-side hiding of the Order control is a courtesy, never the enforcement. Removing the client check must not make the action possible.

## 8.5 — Call-to-action logic (`CAT-04`)

| Viewer | Sees |
|---|---|
| Not signed in | **Apply for Membership** (primary) · **Request a Quote** (secondary) |
| Signed-in member, no registration for this item | **Request this item** — opens intake pre-filled with the item, consuming quote capacity like any other request |
| Signed-in member, active registration | **Order** — at their registered price |
| Member with a paused account | The paused state with what to do next — never a silent failure |
| Admin | The admin view with the assignment control |

**No viewer ever sees an Order control they cannot use.** A refused order after a visible Order button is a P1.

## 8.6 — Ranges and negotiated prices must not contaminate each other

The public range is set by an admin on the item, deliberately. It is **not** derived from any customer's negotiated price, and `ADM-07` must make deriving it impossible rather than merely discouraged. A customer's negotiated price may sit outside the published public range; that is expected and is not an error.

# PART IX — MONEY: CHECKOUT, THE REVIEW GATE, AND THE ORDERS EMAIL

## 9.1 — Order of operations — payment first

This sequence was deliberately reversed back to payment-first. **Do not reorder it.**

```
Member accepts a strategy, or orders a registered catalogue item
   → Checkout (card or ACH bank debit)
   → Funds CONFIRMED  (ACH: settled, not initiated)
   → Centralized orders email fires  — exactly once
   → 24h manufacturer review window OPENS
   → Review CLEARED
   → Production / release
   → Ship
```

## 9.2 — Checkout (`PAY-01`)

Stripe Checkout over the REST API, no SDK dependency. Card and ACH bank debit. Signature-verified, idempotent webhooks.

## 9.3 — ACH semantics (`PAY-02`) — P0 surface

**Confirmed means settled.** An ACH debit in flight:

* opens **no** review window
* sends **no** orders email
* sends **no** receipt
* moves the order to a visible `awaiting settlement` state with an honest explanation to the member

`A09` proves each of these independently, and its broken-input proof must show the check catching a `processing` event that wrongly triggers a side effect.

## 9.4 — Webhook idempotency (`PAY-03`) — P0 surface

A replayed or duplicated event can never double-open a review window, double-send the orders email, double-charge, or double-advance a state. `A10` and `A12` prove it by actually replaying events, not by reading the handler.

## 9.5 — Manual confirmation (`PAY-04`)

Wire and PO: an admin marks funds received, which triggers the **identical** downstream sequence — orders email, review window, state move — through the same code path, not a parallel one.

## 9.6 — Refunds and reversals (`PAY-05`)

Full refunds, partial refunds, and failed-settlement reversals close the review window and mark the order accordingly. No reversed order may remain in the review queue.

## 9.7 — The 24-hour manufacturer review gate (`REV-01`) — the hard constraint

Every order with confirmed funds enters a **mandatory** review window before anything ships. It is a real production-acceptance check by the factory, not a waiting period.

* The window opens at the moment funds are confirmed.
* **`ship_order()` raises if the window has not been cleared. There is no code path around it.** `A11` asserts this by direct call and by attempted route access.
* Reviewer outcomes: **clear** · **hold with reason** · **change request** (spec problem, material availability, capacity conflict, freight problem).
* A change request notifies the member in-portal and by email with what changed and what it costs, and **pauses** the order until they respond.
* A hold that reaches its deadline **escalates to admin** rather than expiring silently.
* Every outcome is attributed to a named reviewer with a timestamp.

## 9.8 — Centralized orders email (`ORD-01`)

Fires to `ORDERS_EMAIL` at the moment funds are **confirmed**, exactly once, with the full order detail. Written to the email log. Idempotent under replay. Never fires on an unsettled ACH debit.

# PART XI — TRUST: PRICING PROVENANCE, SAVINGS, PROOF, AND THE TRANSACTION LEDGER

## 11.1 — Every number traces to an admin input (`DR-08`)

Nothing in the Decision Room is invented. Every price, lead time, MOQ, freight figure, tooling spread, and curve point resolves to a stored input id with a publish timestamp and the admin who entered it. `A15` enforces it; a rendered field with no input id is a P1.

Interpolated values along an entered curve are permitted **and must be labeled as interpolated**. Extrapolation beyond the entered curve is not permitted.

## 11.2 — No general savings claim

The site makes **no** blanket savings claim — no "30–60%" banner, no unsourced comparison. In its place:

**Verified Savings Ledger.** Per member, per item: a **defensible baseline** — their prior supplier's quote, their documented current landed cost, or a sourced market comparable — against the Monti landed cost, with the source of the baseline attached. Totals across items and runs into one number the member can defend internally. **Where no baseline can be sourced, the item shows no savings figure at all.**

**Proof Library / Factory Receipts.** Anonymized public receipts in the place a catalogue would otherwise occupy for social proof: category, quantity, what was made, the landed result, the timeline — with client identity, spec detail, and anything proprietary stripped.

Both are currently unbuilt. Neither may ship in a partial state that implies more evidence than exists. **A written standard for what counts as a defensible baseline is required before the ledger is exposed publicly** — that standard is a Class C item for the Manager.

## 11.3 — Tooling: what the client sees — RESOLVED, BINDING

**This section supersedes every earlier instruction to "cut tooling back" and closes the open decision formerly at §7.4.** It is a specification, not a direction of travel. `DR-06` implements it exactly; `A31` enforces it; `W-DR` certifies it.

The problem being solved: tooling is the single most common cause of a manufacturing buyer feeling ambushed. It is invisible in the unit price, it appears on the first invoice, and it raises an ownership question nobody answered. Hiding it protects nothing — the client finds out on invoice one, at exactly the moment trust matters most. Showing all of it invites haggling over cost structure and teaches the client to negotiate the wrong number. So we show **exactly four facts, always, and never a fifth.**

### 11.3.1 — The Tooling Line

Every item that requires a tool carries a **Tooling Line** in its Decision Room and in its Product Genome. Four facts, in this order, in plain language:

| # | Fact | Example wording | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **What it is** | "One cutting die and four print plates." | Named in the words a non-expert understands. Never a part number alone. |
| **2** | **What it costs** | "$840, one time." | **One number.** Never a build-up, never a range, never "from". |
| **3** | **Who owns it** | "You own it. We hold it and maintain it here." | See §11.3.2. Mandatory on every tooled item, no exceptions. |
| **4** | **How it is being handled in this strategy** | "Spread across this 250,000-unit run — adds $0.003 per unit." *or* "Charged upfront on your first order; unit price unaffected." | Must match the strategy actually being displayed. |

**Never shown to a client, on any surface, under any disposition:** what the tool cost us · Monti's margin on tooling · the amortization schedule internals · tool lifespan or depreciation · the supplier-side cost build-up · any comparison to what another client paid. These live behind `GEN-07`'s admin toggle and are covered by `A15`'s internal-partition rule.

**No fifth fact.** An agent that adds a helpful extra line — a lifespan estimate, a "typical industry cost" comparison, a maintenance schedule — has violated this section. `A31` fails on a fifth field.

### 11.3.2 — Ownership policy — stated the same way every time

**The client owns the tool once it is paid for. We hold it, store it, and maintain it. If they ever want it moved, we release it to them or to another factory at their cost.**

This sentence, or an approved variant of it, appears on every tooled item. It is not buried in terms and it is not conditional.

Why this and not a setup fee we retain: lock-in contradicts the entire commercial thesis. The stated plan is to price low and keep the member because coming back is easier and cheaper than leaving — not because their die is in our building. A client who knows they can leave and stays anyway is the customer this business is built to have. `BRD-01` may not soften this into an implied retention.

Operational consequences that must exist for the statement to be true:

* A tool register: tool id, item, owning customer, paid date, physical location, condition, last used.
* A release procedure with a stated cost and lead time, walked through at least once before launch (`DOC-01`).
* Release is never blocked by a commercial dispute — that is a separate matter and is handled separately.
* Tool ownership appears in the client's **Product Genome logistics section** and in their documents, not only in the Decision Room.

### 11.3.3 — Treatment is a client choice, expressed through the strategies

Tooling treatment is one of the things that **differentiates the three strategies** rather than something the client is told after the fact:

| Strategy | Default tooling treatment |
|---|---|
| **Lowest landed cost** | Amortized across the full displayed run — lowest per-unit, highest first invoice absorbed into the order |
| **Fastest arrival** | Charged upfront so tooling starts immediately; unit price stays clean |
| **Highest certainty** | Charged upfront, with the tool proved out on a sample run before the full quantity releases |

The admin may override any of these per item on the pricing desk. **What the admin may never do is publish a strategy whose tooling treatment is not stated in its Tooling Line** — `A31` catches this.

`DR-07`'s "what would need to change" panel must treat tooling as a lever: *"At 40,000 units the die adds $0.021 per unit. At 250,000 it adds $0.003. Raising the run is the cheapest change available to you."*

### 11.3.4 — The materiality promotion rule

Tooling that is trivial at high volume is severe at low volume, and the same disclosure cannot serve both. For Boars Head's containers this is not hypothetical: an $840 tooling charge is $0.0017 per unit across 500,000 containers and $0.042 per unit across 20,000 — the difference between invisible and **30% of a $0.14 unit price.**

So the rule is proportional:

* **Tooling under 5% of landed unit cost at the displayed quantity** → the Tooling Line renders as a normal line in the cost breakdown.
* **Tooling at or above 5%** → it is **promoted to a callout** above the price, in plain language, before the client can accept the strategy: *"At this quantity, tooling is 30% of your unit cost. It is a one-time charge — your next run of the same item has no tooling cost at all."*
* **The second sentence of that callout is mandatory.** The reason a high tooling percentage is acceptable is that it does not recur, and a client who is not told that reads the number as the real price.

The threshold recomputes live as the quantity slider moves. Crossing it in either direction changes the presentation immediately — `A31` asserts this at boundary quantities.

### 11.3.5 — Reused and existing tools

* An item whose tool already exists shows: *"Tooling already made and paid — no tooling cost on this run."* This is one of the strongest reasons a member's second order is cheaper than their first and it must be **visible**, not silently absorbed into a lower price.
* An item using a tool the client owns from a **previous supplier** shows what condition it arrived in and what, if anything, it needs. Never presented as new tooling.
* A modified tool shows the modification cost as its own Tooling Line with the same four facts.

### 11.3.6 — Class A check `A31` — Tooling disclosure

Add to the §2.2 Class A set:

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| **A31** | **Tooling disclosure** | Every item with a tool renders exactly four Tooling Line facts and no fifth · the ownership sentence is present · the treatment matches the strategy being displayed · zero internal tooling fields appear in any client-facing payload · the materiality callout fires at and above the 5% threshold and not below it, asserted at boundary quantities · a reused tool renders the no-tooling-cost statement |

**Broken-input proof for `A31`:** add a fifth field to one item's Tooling Line and confirm the check names the item and the field; strip the ownership sentence from one item and confirm it is named; set a quantity one unit either side of the 5% threshold and confirm the presentation flips exactly once; then revert all three.

## 11.4 — The transaction ledger — doctrine

**The ledger is the financial record of the business.** It is not a report generated from the order log on demand and it is not a convenience view. It is an append-only record with one immutable row per money event, and everything else — the admin master view, the client view, the three period reports, the exports — is a projection of it.

Three rules govern it absolutely:

1. **Derived, never typed.** A ledger row is written by a money event. No human and no agent creates, edits, or deletes a row. A correction is a new, linked reversing row — never a mutation.
2. **It must reconcile.** Ledger ↔ order log ↔ payment provider must agree, continuously. When they disagree, the break is **named and surfaced**, never smoothed over. `LED-06` may not close a break by adjusting the ledger.
3. **Revenue means settled.** The same rule that governs the review gate governs the ledger: an ACH debit in flight is recorded as *pending*, in its own state, and never as revenue. `A32` proves it.

## 11.5 — The master ledger (admin)

Lives in the admin bay as its own surface (`ADM-13`), implemented by `LED-00…04`.

**Every transaction, in one place.** Charges, settlements, refunds, partial refunds, reversals, manual wire and PO confirmations, and payment-provider fees — each with: receipt number · date and time · customer · order reference · item(s) · quantity · gross · fees · net · payment method (card / ACH / wire / PO) · status · linked review outcome · linked reversing row if any.

### 11.5.1 — Three period formats, all exportable

The same underlying rows, presented three ways. The format selector never changes the data — only the aggregation.

| Format | Shows | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| **Month over Month** | Each calendar month | Current month vs the previous month: gross, net, order count, average order value, refund rate, and the delta on each |
| **Quarter over Quarter** | Each calendar quarter | Current quarter vs the previous quarter, same measures |
| **Year over Year** | Each calendar year | Current year vs the previous year, same measures, plus same-period-last-year for the year in progress |

**`A33` asserts** that all three views sum to the same grand total over the same date range, that period boundaries are handled consistently (inclusive start, exclusive end, one named timezone stored with every row), and that a transaction dated on a boundary lands in exactly one period. A ledger whose month view and quarter view disagree is a P0 — it means the business's own numbers cannot be trusted.

**Every view exports.** The export is the on-screen view: same rows, same columns, same order, same totals, same active filter. `A35` proves it. An export that quietly contains more or fewer rows than the screen is a P1, because it will be pasted into a spreadsheet and reconciled against something.

### 11.5.2 — Search: finding the exact receipt

The master ledger's search is not a convenience — it is how a dispute gets answered. Four search modes, combinable:

| Mode | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Date search** | A single date or a range. Presets for this month, last month, this quarter, this year, and a custom range. The active range drives every period view and every export. |
| **Member search** | By company name, contact name, or customer reference. Selecting a member filters the whole ledger to them and shows their running total. |
| **Order search** | By order reference. Resolves to that order's full transaction chain — the charge, the settlement, any refund, any reversal — in sequence. |
| **Receipt search** | By receipt number. Resolves directly to the single receipt, deep-linkable so it can be sent to a colleague. |

Every result is one click from **the receipt itself** — the document, not a summary of it. `A36` asserts each mode returns the complete matching set and nothing else, against a seeded fixture set with known boundary cases (a transaction on the first second of a month, a refund in a different period from its charge, two members with similar names, a reused-looking order reference).

**A search that silently truncates is a P1.** If a result set is capped, the surface says so and offers the export.

## 11.6 — The client ledger (member portal)

Every member gets their own ledger (`PORT-06`, implemented by `LED-05`): **all of their purchases, in one place, with their own period views, their own search, and their own export.**

Same three formats. Same search modes minus member search, which is meaningless inside a single account. Same one-click access to the receipt.

**What the client ledger shows:** their transactions, their orders, their items and quantities, what they paid, refunds and reversals, running totals, and their period comparisons.

**What it never shows:** anyone else's rows · Monti's fees, margin, or cost build-up · internal reconciliation state · another member's existence in any form.

`A34` proves the tenancy: a cross-tenant ledger query or export returns 404, and — the check that actually matters — **the client's own totals equal the admin master ledger filtered to that customer.** Two ledgers that disagree about what a client paid is the single most damaging inconsistency this product could ship, and it is asserted, not assumed.

The client ledger is also where the **Verified Savings Ledger** (§11.2) surfaces once it exists — the same rows, with the baseline comparison attached. Until that standard is written, the ledger shows what they paid and makes no savings claim.

## 11.7 — Ledger and the payment sequence

The ledger observes §9.1; it does not participate in it. Specifically:

* Checkout started → **no row**.
* Card authorized and captured → **one row**, status `settled`.
* ACH initiated → **one row**, status `pending`, excluded from revenue in every period view and visibly labeled in both ledgers.
* ACH settled → the pending row **transitions** to `settled` (it does not create a second row).
* ACH failed → the pending row transitions to `failed` and a linked reversing row is written if anything downstream had been actioned.
* Manual wire or PO confirmed → **one row**, status `settled`, marked as manually confirmed with the confirming admin named.
* Refund or partial refund → **a new linked row**, never an edit to the original.
* Provider fee → its own row, linked to the transaction it belongs to, visible in the admin ledger and **never** in the client ledger.

`A32` asserts every one of these mappings and that no money event produces zero rows or two rows.

At $0.14–$0.20 per container the materiality rule matters here: verify the 5% callout fires at low quantities and not at high ones, using their real matrix (`A31`).
7. A **tool register entry** per tool: id, item, owning customer, paid date, location, condition, last used.
8. **Ledger rows** for every transaction they have made, reconciling to the order log and the provider, visible in **their** client ledger and in the master ledger, with totals that match (`A32`, `A34`).
9. **`CLI-01` provisioned and scope-verified** (`A30`).

**Quantity range — resolve before Hour 3.** The prototype's Decision Room quantity slider runs **1,000–10,000 units** (Appendix E). At $0.14–$0.20 per container that is a **$140–$2,000 order** — far below any plausible real run for branded carry-out containers, and far below the volume at which the price band reaches $0.14. Shipping the prototype's range against this client's real matrix would make the product look like a toy on its first real order. `ADM-03` sets the slider bounds **from the published price matrix**, per item, not from a hard-coded constant; the bounds are a data field, not a UI decision. The actual Boars Head range is escalated to the user under §18.2 — it is not guessed.

**Open questions to file, not guess.** Any Boars Head detail not stated above — exact SKUs, material, board weight, print process, carton counts, MOQs, lead times, addresses, contacts, incoterm, freight lane — is **escalated to the user under §18.2**, not invented. A client agent that fabricates a spec is a P0.

## Client agent contract (applies to every client, present and future)

**Provisioning.** Creating a customer provisions their agent from the `CLI-00` template. No customer exists without an agent; no agent exists without a customer.

**Scope.** The agent's tool layer is bound to exactly one `customer_id`. The binding is enforced **at the data layer**, not by instruction text. A prompt naming another client returns nothing because the query cannot reach it — not because the agent declines. `A30` proves this, and `CLI-V` attacks it with cross-client prompt injection.

**Job.** For its client: know their items, their price matrix, their run history, their claims and on-time record, their capacity position, and their open decisions. Draft their quote intake. Prepare Decision Room inputs for admin review. Watch their reorder cadence. Flag price-band drift. Answer their questions about their own orders.

**Limits.** A client agent **proposes; it never publishes.** It cannot set or publish a price, change an order state, clear a review, alter a registration, or write to any customer record but its own. Everything it produces enters through the same admin publish step every other input does.

**Lifecycle.** Suspended when the account is paused. Revoked when the account is declined or closed. Re-provisioned on reinstatement. Every lifecycle event logged.

**Adding a client.** Add the row to this appendix → create the customer record → provision the agent → verify scope with `A30` → assign catalogue registrations → build the genome and image set → only then is the client live.

# APPENDIX D — GLOSSARY

**Adversary** — the agent assigned to falsify a claim, distinct from the builder. Certification requires surviving its documented attack.

**Assignment / Registration** — the record binding one catalogue item to one customer at that customer's negotiated price. The thing that makes ordering possible.

**Banned string** — a brand or ownership phrase from §1.5 whose presence anywhere fails `A06`.

**Broken-input proof** — the demonstration that a check fails on a realistic deliberate defect and passes on a known-good input. A check without one does not count.

**Class A / B / C** — machine-decidable / adversarially decidable / judgment-required acceptance criteria.

**Class A coverage** — the share of Class A criteria that have a runnable, proven check. Must reach 100% before G1 closes.

**Client agent** — an AI agent registered to exactly one customer, scoped at the data layer, that proposes but never publishes.

**Decision Room** — the surface that replaced the single flat quote: two rails, three strategies, and controls, all priced off admin inputs.

**Demotion** — moving a Class A criterion to Class B when its check cannot be built. Never to Class C, never to reviewer judgment.

**Evidence laundering** — presenting another agent's conclusion as your own evidence. A P0.

**Fail-closed** — `UNREVIEWED` is treated as `REJECTED`. Absence of review never produces a pass.

**Fixture record** — any seeded, demo, example, or test row. Zero may survive to launch.

**Governance defect** — a process failure listed in §2.8. Always P0. Voids its gate.

**Green drift** — every agent reporting pass while the product is broken.

**Harness rot** — checks quietly weakened mid-run to keep gates green. Detected by re-running proofs at G7 and G12.

**Item** — the unit of the Decision Room. Every quote request becomes one, auto-numbered, renameable by the client forever.

**Mode A / Mode B** — research complete (GO available) versus incomplete (`CONDITIONAL GO` ceiling).

**Number provenance** — the rule that every number a client sees resolves to a stored admin input with a publish timestamp. Enforced by `A15`.

**Order gating** — the server-side refusal, at three points, of any order for an item not registered to the acting member.

**Product Genome** — the manufacturing memory of an approved item: six client-facing sections plus an internal partition.

**Review gate** — the mandatory 24h manufacturer review after funds are confirmed and before anything ships. Enforced in code.

**Settled, not initiated** — the rule that an ACH debit is not confirmed funds until it settles.

**Tenancy** — per-customer data isolation enforced at the data layer, returning 404 rather than 403 on cross-tenant access.

**Tier warden** — the agent holding certification authority for a tier. Never builds. Audited by the Manager and the red team.

**Tripwire** — an automatic, non-waivable governance alarm on a rubber-stamping signature.

**Master ledger** — the admin-facing financial record: every transaction, three period formats, four search modes, exportable. Specified at §11.5.

**Client ledger** — the member-facing projection of the same record, scoped to one customer. §11.6.

**Reconciliation break** — a disagreement between ledger, order log and payment provider. Surfaced by name, never closed by adjusting the ledger.

**Tooling Line** — the four facts shown to a client about a tool — what it is, what it costs, who owns it, how it is being handled in this strategy — and never a fifth. Specified at §11.3, enforced by `A31`.

**Value rung** — an agent's position on the six-level value-escalation ladder.
